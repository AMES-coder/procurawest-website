How to prequalify contractors in Alberta.

Amy-Marie Holland November 19, 2026 7 min read

I wrote a broad overview of contractor prequalification challenges in Alberta earlier this year. That post covered the chaos of managing ISN, Avetta, and ComplyWorks all at once. This one is different. This is the actual how-to - the step-by-step process for building a prequalification program that holds up under audit and keeps your site compliant from day one.

If you have ever awarded work to a contractor only to discover their COR lapsed two months ago, this guide is for you.

Step 1: Define your prequalification criteria

Before you evaluate a single contractor, you need to decide what "qualified" means for your organization. This is where most companies get it wrong - they either set the bar too low and end up managing risk on the job site, or they set it so high that they eliminate half the market and wonder why they only get two bids.

At minimum, your criteria should cover these five areas:

  • Safety certification. In Alberta, that means a valid Certificate of Recognition (COR) or Small Employer Certificate of Recognition (SECOR). No exceptions. If a contractor cannot produce a current COR from an Alberta Partnerships certifying partner, they should not be on your approved vendor list.
  • WCB clearance. A current Workers' Compensation Board clearance letter confirms the contractor is in good standing - meaning their premiums are paid and they are not under a surcharge order. Request this for every contractor, every time.
  • Insurance minimums. Set a floor for commercial general liability - $2 million is standard for most construction and industrial work in Alberta. For higher-risk scopes, push that to $5 million. Require proof of auto liability and professional liability where applicable.
  • Financial stability. For contracts above a certain threshold - say $500,000 - request audited financial statements or at minimum a bank reference letter. A contractor who underbids and then goes insolvent mid-project costs you far more than one who was never on the list.
  • Relevant experience. Define what "relevant" means. Three similar projects in the last five years is a reasonable baseline. Ask for references and actually call them.
The best prequalification programs are specific enough to filter out risk but flexible enough to let qualified new entrants compete. If your approved vendor list never changes, your criteria might be a barrier instead of a standard.

Step 2: Choose the right platform

You do not need a platform if you manage five contractors. You absolutely need one if you manage fifty. Here is how the major options break down in Alberta:

ISNetworld (ISN)

ISN is the dominant platform in oil and gas and heavy industrial. If you work upstream or midstream in Alberta, most of your contractors are already on it. ISN handles document collection, RAVS verification, and safety metrics scoring. The downside is cost - both for you as a hiring client and for your contractors who pay annual subscription fees to maintain their profiles.

Avetta

Avetta is strong in construction, infrastructure, and utilities. It offers similar document management and verification to ISN but tends to be more flexible on configuration. If your work is primarily commercial or institutional construction, Avetta is worth evaluating.

ComplyWorks

ComplyWorks is a Canadian-built platform with deep Alberta roots. It handles COR verification, WCB clearance tracking, and insurance compliance well. For companies that operate primarily within Canada, ComplyWorks often provides better support and more relevant compliance modules than the larger global platforms.

The honest answer is that your choice often comes down to where your contractors already have profiles. If 80% of your supply base is on ISN, forcing them onto Avetta creates friction without adding value.

Step 3: Build your approved vendor list

Your approved vendor list (AVL) is the output of prequalification - the roster of contractors who meet your criteria and are cleared to bid on work. Building it right means more than collecting documents.

  1. Issue a prequalification questionnaire. Send a standardized form to every contractor you want to evaluate. Cover safety, insurance, experience, financial capacity, and any project-specific requirements.
  2. Score each submission against your criteria. Use a weighted scoring matrix. Safety and insurance compliance should carry the heaviest weight. Experience and financial stability come next. Price history or references round it out.
  3. Conduct verification. Do not take documents at face value. Confirm COR status directly through the Alberta Partnerships website. Request WCB clearance letters dated within 30 days. Call the insurance broker to confirm policy limits and effective dates.
  4. Assign a status. Approved, conditionally approved, or not approved. Conditional approval means the contractor meets most criteria but has a gap - maybe their COR audit is due next month. Set a clear deadline and follow up.
1 in 4 Lapsed certification rate In a typical first-round prequalification audit, roughly one in four contractors are found to have at least one lapsed or expiring certification - COR, insurance, or WCB. These are contractors already doing work on site.

Step 4: Track expiry dates and renewals

This is where programs fall apart. The prequalification is done, the list is built, and then nobody tracks when the documents expire. Six months later, you have contractors on site with lapsed COR certificates and expired insurance - and you do not know it until an auditor or an incident forces you to look.

Every document you collect should be logged with its expiry date. Set automated reminders at 60 days and 30 days before expiry. If you are using ISN, Avetta, or ComplyWorks, the platform handles this. If you are running a spreadsheet, build the reminder system yourself - calendar alerts, conditional formatting, whatever it takes.

Assign ownership. Someone in your organization needs to be responsible for following up on expiring documents. If that person does not exist, the program will decay within a year.

Step 5: Know what to do when a contractor fails

A contractor who was approved last year might not meet your criteria today. COR certificates lapse. Insurance policies get cancelled. WCB surcharges appear. Financial problems develop. Here is how to handle it:

  • Immediate suspension for safety-critical gaps. If a COR certificate or WCB clearance lapses, the contractor should be suspended from active work until the gap is resolved. This is not negotiable.
  • Conditional notice for correctable issues. If an insurance certificate is expiring within 30 days and the contractor has a renewal in progress, issue a conditional notice with a firm deadline. Document the conversation.
  • Removal for sustained non-compliance. If a contractor cannot or will not resolve a compliance gap within your stated timeline, remove them from the AVL. Notify the contractor in writing and document your rationale. This protects you if the decision is ever questioned.

The goal is never to be punitive. It is to maintain a standard. Contractors who want to work with you will meet the requirements. Those who cannot are telling you something about how they manage their business.

Putting it all together

A contractor prequalification program does not have to be complicated. It needs to be consistent. Define your criteria once. Choose a platform that fits your volume. Build the list with real verification. Track renewals with reminders that have teeth. And handle failures with clear, documented processes.

The companies that do this well rarely think about it - because the system runs and the right contractors show up. The companies that skip it spend their time managing incidents, audit findings, and compliance gaps that were entirely preventable.
