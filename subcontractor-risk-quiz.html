2-Minute Risk Check

Is your subcontractor in financial trouble before they tell you?

Thin margins and rising input costs put real pressure on subcontractors. These are seven observable warning signs - no accounting degree required.

Takes about 2 minutes. Based on common early warning signs of contractor financial stress.

For project managers and procurement teams managing active subcontracts in O&G and construction.

Question 1 of 70%
01
Has your subcontractor recently asked for payment acceleration, larger upfront deposits, or advanced billing on work not yet complete?

A financially healthy contractor bills on the normal schedule. Requests to pull cash forward are a classic early signal.

02
Have you noticed delays in invoice submission, slow responses to payment inquiries, or inconsistent communication from their office?

Administrative breakdown is often the first visible sign of a contractor under internal financial stress.

03
Is your project their primary or only active contract right now?

A contractor dependent on one revenue stream has no buffer if this project goes sideways. Single-client dependency is a structural risk.

04
Has their crew size, crew quality, or equipment condition declined noticeably since the contract was awarded?

Replacing experienced workers with cheaper labor, or running older equipment past its service cycle, signals a company cutting costs it can no longer afford.

05
Has their WCB coverage, liability insurance, or bonding recently lapsed, been reduced, or been flagged for non-renewal?

Insurance and WCB premiums are one of the first things a cash-strapped contractor lets slide. A lapse here is a late-stage warning sign.

06
Have they asked to substitute specified materials or equipment for cheaper alternatives mid-project, without a clear technical reason?

Substitution requests that save money without improving performance are a cost-reduction signal, not an engineering decision.

07
Have you heard through your network - from other operators, suppliers, or industry contacts - that this contractor may be having cash flow or financial problems?

The Alberta O&G and construction industry is small. Network talk isn't proof, but it's often an early signal worth checking out.
