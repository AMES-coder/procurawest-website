12-Point MSA Audit

Is your services contract inflation-proof?

US construction input prices ran at a 12.6% annualized pace in January-February 2026 (ABC analysis of BLS data), with steel and aluminum leading the climb. If your MSA has no escalation language, you're absorbing those costs with no contractual recourse.

Rate & Cost Protection
01
Your MSA has a defined escalation clause allowing rate adjustments over the contract term.
The baseline. Without this clause, your contractor has no formal mechanism to request rate changes - and neither do you.
02
The escalation clause is tied to a specific, published index - such as Statistics Canada's Industrial Product Price Index (IPPI).
Vague escalation language ("market rates may change") is hard to apply and easy to dispute. Index-linked language is objective and auditable.
03
The escalation clause includes a cap - a maximum percentage increase allowed in any given review period.
An uncapped escalation clause protects the contractor, not you. A cap gives both sides a known ceiling for each review period.
04
The contract specifies a formal rate review schedule - either annual or triggered by defined market events.
Without a review schedule, your contractor can request a rate increase at any time. A fixed schedule means both parties know when it's coming.
Tariff & Cost Pass-Through
05
Your contract includes a tariff or duty cost pass-through provision with defined limits on what can be passed and how it's documented.
Without limits, a contractor can pass any input cost increase directly to you and call it a tariff. Documentation requirements create accountability.
06
The agreement includes a change-of-law clause covering new import duties, tariffs, or government-mandated cost increases.
Tariffs are enacted by governments mid-contract. A change-of-law clause determines who bears those costs and under what conditions.
07
Material substitutions require your written approval - contractors can't swap specified materials for cheaper alternatives without your sign-off.
In an inflationary market, contractors cut costs wherever they can. A substitution-approval clause keeps your spec integrity intact.
Audit & Benchmarking Rights
08
Your contract includes audit rights - you can request documentation to verify that a rate increase or cost pass-through is legitimate.
Without audit rights, you're taking your contractor's word for what their costs are. Audit rights don't mean you'll use them - they mean you can.
09
The agreement includes a Most-Favored Customer (MFC) clause - your contractor can't charge you more than their best rates offered to comparable clients.
Rare in small-operator MSAs but extremely valuable. If your contractor is discounting for volume clients, you should know.
Flexibility & Exit
10
The contract specifies the billing currency and includes a mechanism to address exchange rate changes on cross-border services or materials.
Relevant for any US-denominated service or equipment component. A 5% swing on USD/CAD can wipe out your escalation cap.
11
Your MSA includes a termination-for-convenience clause - you can exit without cause with reasonable notice, so you're not locked in if rates become unacceptable.
An MSA without a termination-for-convenience clause is a trap. You need the ability to walk away if a rate increase negotiation fails.
12
This MSA has been reviewed by someone with procurement expertise in the last 12 months.
A contract that hasn't been reviewed since 2023 was written for a stable market. The past 18 months have changed what "standard language" should look like.
